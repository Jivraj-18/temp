What happens after a filing is published

A filing is not final. Firms return months or years later and change what they already reported. There is no corrections feed, no notification, and nothing in the new document says which filing it replaces.

The correction says new holdings — add these, keep the rest. Handle it as a replacement, which is the obvious default, and $4.0tn of holdings disappear 7 days after they were first reported.

Across the quarter: assuming corrections replace wrongly deletes $7.5tn; assuming they add wrongly double-counts $9.5tn. One field decides which, and 2.8% of corrections do not state it at all.

What this demonstrates

  • Ingestion is fast and provably so — 32,134 holdings across 118 filings in 626 ms, a flat 19.5 microseconds per holding regardless of filing size.
  • Identifiers resolve at 83.9% from free sources on a random sample, with the failures published rather than rounded away — and SEDOL, which our proposal promised, cannot be delivered without a licence.
  • The hard part is time, not parsing. Holdings history is rewritten after publication. A store that records only which quarter a fact belongs to — and not when it was learned — is wrong and cannot tell you so.
  • No AI was used anywhere in this pipeline. The filings are structured XML. Adding a model would add cost and risk without adding accuracy.

Method. 118 of 1,730 13F-HR filings from 2026-05-15, downloaded from SEC EDGAR and parsed. Identifiers resolved via OpenFIGI and the SEC issuer crosswalk. Corrections analysis covers all 318 13F-HR/A filings from 1 April to 30 June 2026, each matched to the filing it corrects; 24 had no retrievable original and are excluded.

Limits. One quarter, US filings only. 13F covers long US-listed equity — no shorts, no non-US listings, no bonds. Amendment chains are not modelled. The value figures are gross exposure under each wrong rule, not realised loss.

Public SEC data throughout. Every figure regenerates from source with one command.
